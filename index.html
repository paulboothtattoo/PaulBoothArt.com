About Paul Booth

Artist. Tattooist. Sculptor. Filmmaker.

For nearly four decades, Paul Booth has built a singular body of work at the intersection of dark surrealism, psychological symbolism, Gothic aesthetics, and uncompromising craftsmanship.

His imagery is immediately recognizable, yet it has never been confined to a single medium. Booth approaches art as a complete language—one capable of moving from skin to canvas, from sculpture to film, from sound to physical space without losing its identity.

Read the full biography

Best known internationally for his transformative influence on contemporary tattooing, Booth helped push the medium far beyond conventional decoration. His highly detailed black-and-gray work brought the atmosphere, depth, composition, and emotional intensity of fine art to the human body. Distorted anatomy, biomechanical forms, ritual symbolism, decaying figures, and psychologically charged imagery became part of a visual vocabulary that would influence generations of tattoo artists around the world.

But tattooing has always been only one part of a much larger practice. Booth works fluidly across painting, drawing, tattooing, sculpture, filmmaking, photography, music, design, digital media, and immersive installation. Rather than treating these disciplines as separate careers, he uses each medium as another instrument for exploring the same evolving artistic universe. A form that begins as a drawing may become a tattoo, a sculptural object, a cinematic image, an environment, or a piece of moving digital art. The medium changes; the underlying language remains unmistakably his own.

Across this work, Booth repeatedly returns to the darker territories of human experience: mortality, fear, transformation, identity, ritual, isolation, consciousness, and the unconscious. Darkness is not used simply for shock or spectacle. It becomes a means of investigation—a way of confronting the psychological forces that exist beneath the surface of everyday life. His figures often appear suspended between states: human and monstrous, biological and mechanical, sacred and profane, living and decaying. The grotesque becomes a mirror rather than an endpoint.

That philosophy extended beyond individual artworks into the environments Booth created around them. His Last Rites Tattoo Theatre & Gallery in New York became a physical manifestation of his artistic world—a place where tattooing, painting, sculpture, music, performance, architecture, and atmosphere could coexist. More than a studio or gallery, it demonstrated Booth’s ability to think not only in terms of individual objects, but in complete experiences.

Sculpture allowed that world to become tangible. Film introduced movement, time, sound, and narrative. Music added another emotional dimension. Painting and drawing offered unrestricted spaces for anatomy, texture, symbolism, and psychological abstraction. Throughout each discipline, Booth has maintained the same underlying commitment: to create work that feels discovered rather than manufactured—as though the image, object, or environment has been unearthed from some deeper internal mythology.

His career has therefore been defined as much by versatility as consistency. Few artists move comfortably between so many forms while maintaining such a distinct visual identity. Whether working with a tattoo machine, paint, clay, a camera, sound, physical materials, or emerging digital tools, Booth treats technique as secondary to vision. The medium serves the idea, not the other way around.

Today, his practice continues to evolve across both physical and digital forms, extending decades of traditional craftsmanship into new technologies and experimental methods of image-making. What remains constant is the desire to make the invisible visible—to give physical form to fear, memory, transformation, imagination, and the hidden architecture of the psyche.

Paul Booth’s work is not defined by a single medium. It is the construction of an interconnected world—built across skin, canvas, objects, sound, moving image, and space.
